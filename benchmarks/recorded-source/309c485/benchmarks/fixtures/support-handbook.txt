Atlas operations

Atlas is the synthetic Frankfurt service used in this handbook. Its support handover owner is Nora Chen. The maintenance window is Tuesday from 02:00 to 03:00 UTC. Atlas change requests must be announced 36 hours before maintenance starts. The operator records the ticket number, rollback owner and affected region. Customers receive a status note when maintenance begins and another when normal service returns. Emergency changes use the incident procedure rather than this routine window. All names, services and procedures in this handbook are fictional benchmark data.

Beacon operations

Beacon is a separate synthetic Berlin service. Its support handover owner is Elias Reed. The maintenance window is Thursday from 04:00 to 05:00 UTC. Beacon change requests must be announced 48 hours before maintenance starts. These settings belong only to Beacon and must not be used for Atlas. An operator checks the ticket scope and backup record before the change. Routine updates are documented in the service journal. Emergency work follows the incident procedure. This fictional service exists partly to make similarly worded records distinguishable in retrieval.

Atlas billing adjustments

The fictional Atlas plan allows a credit adjustment for duplicate processing charges reported within 14 days of the invoice date. The customer supplies the invoice identifier and duplicate transaction reference. The billing reviewer records an explanation and links the original invoice. The adjustment does not change the maintenance window or the support handover owner. Beacon uses a separate billing policy and its record should not be substituted for this Atlas procedure. This text is synthetic testing material and describes no real commercial policy.

Incident response

For a severity-one Atlas incident, the on-call engineer must acknowledge the alert within 15 minutes. The engineer then posts the incident reference in the ops-incidents channel and identifies an incident lead. A second engineer checks rollback safety before any change. Severity-two incidents have an acknowledgement target of 60 minutes and do not automatically require the same notification channel. Customer-facing status messages describe verified impact and the next update time. Internal notes preserve uncertain findings as uncertain. The incident ends only after recovery has been observed and the incident lead has recorded the outcome.

Data exports

Atlas audit exports use JSONL format. Each line is one complete event with an event identifier, UTC timestamp and service name. Export files expire after 180 days. A new export is created for a changed date range; a previous export is never silently edited. The operator checks both the service and the time range before sharing a file. Beacon debug snapshots use CSV and expire after seven days, so those values must not be confused with the Atlas audit export rules. Export expiration is separate from retention of the original source logs.
---
Sign-in sessions

An Atlas staff sign-in session expires after four hours of inactivity. Signing in again creates a new session identifier. The fictional system does not store a payroll administrator password in this handbook. Service operators can inspect the session timestamp but cannot recover a password from a document. Beacon staff sessions expire after two hours of inactivity, which is a different service-specific rule. A user who changes teams must have their service access reviewed separately. Access review is independent of maintenance scheduling and billing adjustments.
---
Backups and restores

Atlas backups start daily at 02:30 UTC and are retained for 21 days. A restore exercise runs every Monday. The engineer checks a restored sample against the recorded manifest and writes the result in the service journal. A failed restore exercise remains an open issue until a later exercise passes and the reviewer records its scope. Beacon backups start at 01:00 UTC and are retained for 14 days. Atlas and Beacon manifests must never be merged, because doing so would obscure which service was actually restored.
---
Evidence retention

Atlas signed audit reports are retained for 365 days. Raw diagnostic logs are retained for 30 days. These are different artifacts: the signed report contains the reviewed findings, while the raw logs contain the original events. When a report cites a raw log, it also records the event identifier and capture time. Export files follow their own 180-day expiration rule. The handbook does not provide transport prices, travel schedules or personal financial information. Questions about those topics cannot be answered from this fictional document.
